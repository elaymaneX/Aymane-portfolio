The SaaS model has been the backbone of tech for over a decade. But there's a fundamental shift happening that most people in the industry are underestimating.

## The Problem with SaaS

Every SaaS product is essentially a UI wrapper around a set of operations. You log in, click buttons, fill forms, and the software does the thing. But here's the catch — **you're still doing most of the work**. The software just makes it slightly less painful.

Think about it: CRMs, project management tools, email marketing platforms — they all require you to manually input data, configure workflows, and monitor dashboards. The "service" part of SaaS is really just hosting and maintenance.

## Enter Agentic AI

What if instead of clicking through Salesforce, you just told an agent: "Follow up with every lead that went cold in the last 30 days, personalize the message based on their last interaction, and book a meeting if they respond positively"?

That's not science fiction. With tool-calling LLMs, multi-agent orchestration frameworks like LangGraph, and the explosion of APIs, this is buildable **today**.

## Why This Matters

The implications are massive:

1. **Pricing shifts from seats to outcomes** — Why pay per user when an agent can do the work of 5?
2. **UI becomes optional** — Dashboards become summaries, not workspaces.
3. **Integration becomes trivial** — Agents can bridge tools that were never meant to talk to each other.
4. **Customization goes from months to minutes** — Describe what you want in natural language.

## The Transition Won't Be Instant

There are real challenges: reliability, hallucination, trust. Enterprise buyers won't switch overnight. But the economic pressure is enormous — if an AI agent can do 80% of what a $50/seat/month tool does for $5, the market will move.

## What I'm Building

This is exactly why I've been deep in multi-agent systems and automation workflows. My [Automated Job Tracker](/projects) is a small example — an n8n workflow that replaces what would normally be a dedicated SaaS product. And I think this pattern scales to almost every category.

The companies that will win the next decade aren't building better UIs. They're building better agents.
